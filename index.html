  • English Premier League Data Machine Learning Analysis

    Machine learning analysis of English Premier League data for predictive insights. English Premier League Data Machine Learning Analysis This project was the capstone for my Intro to Machine Learning course at the University of Maryland. Over the span of a semester, I worked independently to analyze English Premier League (EPL) data using various machine learning techniques with the goal of generating predictive insights—particularly match outcomes and team performance metrics. I selected this dataset because of my interest in both sports analytics and practical applications of ML. I began by cleaning and organizing several seasons' worth of EPL data, converting categorical variables into numerical ones, and engineering features to account for metrics such as home advantage, goal differential, and possession percentage. I then applied a variety of models—logistic regression, decision trees, random forests, and k-nearest neighbors—to determine which approach yielded the best predictive accuracy. Cross-validation and model tuning were essential parts of my workflow to avoid overfitting and ensure generalizability. This project demonstrates my ability to handle an end-to-end data science process: from data wrangling and visualization to model building and performance evaluation. It also reflects my capacity to work independently, manage a long-term technical project, and apply theoretical ML concepts in a meaningful, real-world context. My work on this project strengthened my proficiency in Python (Pandas, scikit-learn, Matplotlib) and reinforced my motivation to pursue data-driven problem solving in both academic and professional settings.

  • Web Scraping for Indigenous Tribal Data

    Developed tools to gather health data on over 6,500 Indigenous Tribal Groups.
    My work at the Indian Health Service at the United States Department of Health and Human Services. Web Scraping for Indigenous Tribal Health Data – Indian Health Service This project was completed during my remote internship with the Indian Health Service (IHS) through the Virtual Student Federal Service (VSFS) program. My objective was to develop tools to collect and organize public health data relevant to over 6,500 Indigenous Tribal groups in the United States. This effort supported IHS initiatives aimed at improving health equity and informing policy decisions for underserved communities. The project involved writing custom web scraping scripts in Python using libraries such as Selenium, BeautifulSoup, and pandas. I automated the retrieval of publicly available health records, population data, and tribal classifications across numerous government and nonprofit websites. One major challenge was ensuring that the scripts handled a wide variety of webpage structures and remained resilient to minor layout changes—a task that required careful use of XPath, exception handling, and dynamic selectors. In addition to scraping and cleaning data, I contributed to organizing the information into structured formats that allowed for basic analytics and mapping. This improved accessibility and usability for public health officials and researchers who previously had to gather this data manually. This project reflects my ability to apply technical skills to meaningful, mission-driven work. It also strengthened my understanding of ethical data collection practices, especially when working with information tied to historically marginalized communities. Overall, it showcases both my programming abilities and my commitment to using technology for social impact.

  • Business Proposal: AI Chatbots Assistance to Enhance Student Learning

    Developed a through business proposal analyzing the feasability and appraoch to implement AI chatbots as study aids in specific courses at the University of Maryland. AI Chatbots for Personalized Student Support at the University of Maryland This business proposal was developed for a professional writing course at the University of Maryland, with a focus on applying emerging technologies to improve student outcomes. The primary audience for this proposal is the University’s Division of Information Technology, with secondary and tertiary audiences including the Division of Student Affairs, faculty, and students. Gatekeepers such as the Office of General Counsel were also considered in the proposal’s structure and feasibility. The problem addressed is a common challenge faced by students: the lack of personalized academic support and the overwhelming task of navigating extensive course materials. Many students struggle to locate specific information in class resources such as lecture slides, textbooks, or supplementary documents, especially across multiple courses. My proposed solution is the implementation of an AI-powered chatbot integrated into ELMS-Canvas for each course with substantial content. These chatbots would be trained exclusively on course materials and provide on-demand support for students by surfacing relevant definitions, explanations, or lecture topics. A similar tool has already been tested in CMSC335 at UMD, offering a viable precedent for broader adoption. This proposal integrates technical feasibility with institutional relevance, aligning with UMD’s goal of using digital tools to improve educational access and student success. I used a strategy grounded in logical reasoning (logos), citing sources on digital learning and personalized education. Through this work, I demonstrated my ability to connect technological solutions with real-world institutional needs, reinforcing my interest in educational innovation and AI applications.
